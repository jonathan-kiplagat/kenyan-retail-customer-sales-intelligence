# Customer and Sales Intelligence for a Kenyan Retailer

**Final Technical Report: LuxDevHQ Data Science and Analytics Capstone**

| | |
|---|---|
| Author | Jonathan Kiplagat |
| Client (fictional) | Duka Bora Supermarkets: 6 stores in Nairobi CBD, Westlands, Thika, Mombasa, Kisumu and Nakuru |
| Period analysed | 1 January 2024 – 31 December 2025 |
| Currency | Kenyan shillings (KES) |
| Repository | github.com/jonathan-kiplagat/kenyan-retail-customer-sales-intelligence |

> **Data notice:** all retail records are synthetic, generated by a seeded and documented script. The only real-world

> real Kenyan retailers.

---

## 1. Executive summary

Duka Bora records every sale but decides stock, promotions and retention on intuition. This project turned two years
of sales (3,000 raw rows → 2,988 clean lines, 2,650 receipts, 1,788 identified customers) into evidence for four
management questions.

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| **What drives revenue and profit?** | Revenue reached **KES 2.18M** at a **24.7% margin**. 2025 revenue grew **8.8%**, but **units fell 4.7%**: growth came from prices and basket mix, not volume. **Clothing earns 46% of profit** on 27% of revenue. Groceries earn 25% of revenue but only 12% of profit. |
| **Who buys, where and when?** | December peaks and January troughs every year. Saturdays and evenings are busiest. **The top 20% of customers produce 61% of revenue**, but only 23% of customers ever return. Nairobi CBD and Westlands produce 44% of revenue. Thika is the most space-efficient store but is shrinking. |
| **Which customers are drifting away?** | **249 of 419 established customers** (KES 542K of lifetime spend) have been silent for more than 180 days. On later data, the 180-day rule identified non-returners with **93% precision** (AUC 0.80). |
| **What demand should we expect?** | The selected model forecasts an underlying level of **KES 99K** for January 2026 (80% range KES 82–121K). January is seasonally weak, so we recommend **planning for KES 75–85K**. |

**Top three recommendations:**
1. Protect clothing margins, especially school uniforms.
2. Cap promotional discounts below each category's margin.
3. Run a controlled win-back campaign for the 249 at-risk customers, highest value first.

## 3. Data

| Table | Rows | Content |
|---|---:|---|
| `sales_transactions` | 3,000 raw / 2,988 clean | One product line per receipt: date, time, store, customer, product, quantity, price, discount, payment |
| `customers` | 1,800 | Gender, age band, county, registration date, loyalty membership, preferred store (no personal identifiers) |
| `products` | 40 | Six categories: Food and Groceries, Beverages, Household Products, Personal Care, Clothing, Electronics |
| `stores` | 6 | Town, county, format, floor area |
| `promotions` | 20 | Category campaigns with fixed discounts |
| `calendar` | 731 | Weekend, payday-window and public-holiday flags |
| Public holidays (real) | 28 | From the Public Holidays Act and Kenya Gazette notices, verified per row |

**Generation design.** The customers carry hidden behaviour segments (78% one-off, 13% occasional, 6.5% regular,
2.5% loyal) that are never exported, plus "drifters" who stop buying. Demand follows realistic seasonality, weekday,
payday and holiday effects, and there is a 4% price review in January 2025. Full detail:
[methodology.md](methodology.md#2-data-acquisition-synthetic-data-by-design-milestone-1).

## 4. Data preparation

**126 realistic data-quality issues** were injected and logged during generation. Cleaning found **all 126**.

| Issue | Rows | Treatment |
|---|---:|---|
| Exact duplicate rows | 12 | Removed |
| Dates in DD/MM/YYYY format | 15 | Parsed with an explicit format |
| Totals stored as text ("KES 1,234.00") | 10 | Converted to numbers |
| Payment label variants | 30 | Mapped to 4 standard labels |
| Missing payment method | 10 | 3 recovered from the same receipt; 7 labelled "Unknown" |
| Missing customer ID | 25 | 4 recovered from the same receipt; 21 flagged as walk-in |
| Quantity 999 (entry error) | 4 | Restored from the receipt total. **Would have added KES 1.44M of phantom revenue** |
| Unit price 10× shelf price | 3 | Replaced with the usual price |
| Total saved without the discount | 6 | Recalculated |
| Genuine bulk purchases (2) and high-value IQR outliers (356) | — | **Kept**: they reconcile with their totals |

**The rule was evidence before assumption.** A missing value was filled only when the same receipt proved it;
otherwise it was labelled. All money columns were recalculated from their base fields, so every line reconciles. The
only remaining differences from the true data are the 28 values that had no evidence, which is the correct outcome.

## 5. Findings

### 5.1 Sales performance

| KPI | 2024–2025 |
|---|---:|
| Net revenue | KES 2,179,215 |
| Gross profit | KES 538,802 (24.7%) |
| Receipts | 2,650 |
| Average order value (median) | KES 822 (KES 450) |
| Revenue growth 2025 vs 2024 | +8.8% |
| Units sold, 2025 vs 2024 | −4.7% |

- **Growth is price-led.** Profit grew 10.7% and orders 3.8%, while units fell. The 4% price review and a richer
  basket mix explain the gap. Units and orders need tracking alongside revenue.
- **Seasonality is strong and predictable.** December is the best month (KES 119K in 2024, KES 141K in 2025) and
  January the weakest (KES 65K and 70K). Holiday eves average 5.2 orders per day vs 3.2 on ordinary weekdays.
- **Daily rhythm.** Peaks run 17:00–19:59 and at lunchtime. Saturday averages 4.5 orders per day; Monday is the
  slowest at 3.0.

### 5.2 Products and categories

| Category | Share of revenue | Share of profit | Margin |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Clothing | 27% | **46%** | 42% |
| Food and Groceries | 25% | 12% | 12% |

- **The top five profit products are all clothing,** led by the school uniform set (KES 60.9K profit). The
  smartphone is the 6th most profitable product, at a 16% margin.
- **Groceries generate traffic, not profit.** They make up the most lines (1,106) but have a thin margin.
- **Some promotions give the margin away.** Grocery campaigns (10% off a 12.6% margin) and Black Friday electronics
  (20% off a 19% margin) kept only 1–3% margin, and 16 promotion lines sold at a loss.

### 5.3 Customers
- **Revenue is concentrated.** The top 20% of customers produce 61% of revenue, and the top 10% produce 43%.
- **Retention is low.** 23.4% of customers return. One-off customers are 77% of customers but only 52% of revenue.
  After their first quarter, typically 8–16% of a cohort comes back.
- **RFM segments.** 68 **Champions** (3.8% of customers) produce 14.7% of revenue. A further 55 formerly frequent
  customers (**Cannot Lose Them** 31, **At Risk** 24) have gone quiet, with KES 230K of past spend.
- **Loyalty membership is associated with returning:** a 41.7% repeat rate for members vs 14.7% for non-members.
  This is not proof that membership causes retention.
- **Customers are loyal to their home store:** 87.8% of identified orders are placed at the customer's preferred
  store.

### 5.4 Stores

| Store | Revenue (KES) | Margin | Avg order value | 2025 growth | Revenue per m² |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Nairobi CBD | 525,975 | 24.3% | 818 | +16.9% | 219 |
| Westlands | 433,184 | 26.0% | 899 | +8.7% | 206 |
| Nakuru Town | 335,755 | 25.5% | 905 | −8.6% | 240 |
| Kisumu Town | 317,705 | 22.5% | 892 | +38.2% | 244 |
| Mombasa Town | 309,861 | 25.5% | 677 | +11.5% | 194 |
| Thika | 256,735 | 24.2% | 755 | −14.2% | **285** |

- **Single electronics sales distort store growth.** Without electronics, Kisumu's +38% becomes +3.5%, Nakuru's −9%
  becomes +11%, and Thika's decline deepens to −19%.
- **Thika is the most efficient store per m², but it is genuinely shrinking.** It also has the highest share of
  at-risk customers (4.6%).
- **Mombasa has busy traffic (458 receipts, third of six) but the smallest baskets** and the lowest revenue per m².

## 6. Statistical evidence

| Question | Test (chosen after assumption checks) | Result | Effect size | Business meaning |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Do promotion lines sell more units? | Mann-Whitney U, one-sided; per category with Holm correction | p = 6.7×10⁻¹¹; significant in **6 of 6** categories | +0.49 units per line (95% CI 0.21–0.93); r = 0.19 | A real but small uplift. It does not pay for discounts larger than the margin |
| Does order value differ by store? | Kruskal-Wallis; Welch ANOVA on log values | p = 0.017 (Welch log p = 0.008) | ε² = 0.003 | Real but tiny. Only Mombasa vs Westlands differ (median KES 411 vs 480) |
| Do categories differ in units per line? | Kruskal-Wallis | p ≈ 10⁻⁵² | ε² = 0.083 | Multi-buy offers suit beverages and groceries |
| Does payment method depend on category? | Chi-square test | Holm p = 0.043 | Cramér's V = 0.056 | Electronics buyers need card and bank options |
| Does time of day depend on category? | Chi-square test | p = 0.33 | V = 0.043 | One staffing plan fits every category |

Non-parametric tests were used because units and order values are skewed and discrete (Shapiro-Wilk p < 0.001 in
every group). The raw ANOVA on store order values (p = 0.076) is reported to show why test choice must follow the
assumption checks. No causal claims are made: promotions were not randomly assigned. Details:
[milestone_5_statistics/README.md](../milestone_5_statistics/README.md).

## 7. Predictive models

Full evaluation: [model_evaluation_report.md](model_evaluation_report.md).

### 7.1 Demand forecast
- **Set-up:** walk-forward, one month ahead. Train on 2024, validate on January–June 2025, test on July–December
  2025.
- **Selected model:** damped Holt exponential smoothing. It had the lowest validation MAE and also ranked first on
  test: **MAE KES 17,500 (16.5%)**, vs 18,448 for the naive baseline and 17,678 for the Random Forest.
- **Honest assessment:** a 5% improvement over the naive baseline on six months is not conclusive. Most of the test
  error is the December 2025 peak (under-forecast by KES 48K), because two years of data cannot support a validated
  seasonal model.
- **January 2026 forecast:**
  - Point forecast: **KES 98,788**.
  - 80% range: KES 82,007–120,796.
  - Seasonal scenario: KES 73,442.
  - **Planning figure: KES 75–85K.**

### 7.2 At-risk customers
- **Definition:** an established customer (2+ orders) with no purchase in the next 6 months. The 180-day threshold
  is the 90th percentile of repeat-purchase gaps (173 days).
- **Candidates:** a recency rule, logistic regression and a Random Forest, all compared on an **out-of-time** test.
  - **Recency rule (selected):** test AUC 0.80, precision 0.93, recall 0.67.
  - **Logistic regression:** AUC 0.83, a small but significant improvement (+0.037, CI +0.002 to +0.073). It is
    used as a priority score.
  - **Random Forest:** overfit (train AUC 0.96 vs test 0.82).
- **Current list:** 249 customers, KES 541,641 of lifetime spend, on dashboard page 3.

## 10. Limitations
- **The data is synthetic.** The patterns are realistic by design, but they are not market evidence.
- **Two years of history.** No validated seasonal forecast is possible, and there are only six test months.
- **Observational data.** Promotion and loyalty effects are associations.
- **Small churn samples.** Only 33 customers returned in the training snapshot. Probabilities are not calibrated.
- **Missing external drivers.** There is no data on competitors, stock-outs, prices elsewhere or macroeconomics.
  KNBS inflation and census data were removed from scope at the project owner's request.

## 11. Future work
1. **Add a seasonal forecast model** (Holt-Winters or SARIMA) once three years of data exist, and forecast at
   category level for stock planning.
2. **Run randomized promotion and win-back trials** to measure causal uplift.
3. **Add a basket analysis** (association rules) to design the Mombasa bundles.
4. **Calibrate the churn model** and estimate customer lifetime value, so retention spend can be prioritized.
5. **Automate the pipeline,** with scheduled refreshes from the database to Power BI.
